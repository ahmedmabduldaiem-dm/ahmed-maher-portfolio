Why sitemap coverage is more than a URL count

Compare intended URLs with crawl findings before making an indexation claim.

Educational article · Published 24 October 2026 · Sources checked 24 October 2026 · 2 min read

Start with the decision

A sitemap audit should help a team decide which URLs belong in search and what needs attention. A total URL count alone cannot answer that question. Google describes a sitemap as a discovery aid; inclusion does not guarantee crawling or indexing.

Create one review table

My suggested workflow is to join the submitted URL list with a crawl at URL level. Keep the submitted URL, response status, final destination, canonical target, robots directives, content type and internal discovery status in separate fields. Record the crawl date and the sitemap version so another reviewer can reproduce the comparison.

Separate the findings

An absent URL may be intentionally excluded. A submitted URL may redirect, duplicate another page or be ineligible for indexing. A discovered URL is not proof of indexation. Name these situations separately, then use Search Console evidence where available to investigate the search-engine side.

A practical example

Imagine a sitemap with a product URL that redirects to a newer page. Record both URLs rather than counting them as two unique product opportunities. Ask whether the destination is the intended search page, whether internal links agree, and whether the sitemap needs an update. This is an illustrative example, not a client result.

Close the loop

Assign an owner to each actionable finding. Retain a before snapshot, re-crawl after implementation and document what changed. Report “sitemap corrected” only after checking the output; reserve “indexation improved” for evidence of that separate outcome.
