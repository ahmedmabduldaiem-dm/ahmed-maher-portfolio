Start keyword planning with a useful audience question

Connect the search question to a page purpose before optimizing individual words.

Educational article · Published 24 October 2026 · Sources checked 24 October 2026 · 2 min read

Begin with a person and a decision

Keyword research is more useful when it starts with what someone is trying to understand or decide. A phrase can indicate comparison, education, navigation or action. My suggested planning method is to write the question in plain language before assigning it to a page.

Give the page a clear job

Google’s starter guidance frames SEO around helping search engines understand content and helping people discover it. For a content brief, translate that principle into a specific reader outcome: what should someone be able to understand or do after reading? A list of related keywords cannot replace that decision.

Map the journey

Consider an illustrative visitor comparing service options. An overview page might help them understand the choices, while a focused service page explains suitability and the next action. Do not force every query into the same landing page. Record where the visitor comes from and what useful step follows.

Write a reviewable brief

Specify the audience question, page purpose, required subject expertise, important evidence and internal links. Leave space for natural language. Titles and headings should communicate the subject clearly rather than repeat a phrase mechanically. Give content owners a way to identify factual gaps before writing begins.

Evaluate usefulness and performance

A completed page needs an editorial review as well as technical checks. Once it is available, compare the observed search queries with the intended purpose and look at the appropriate next action. If expectations differ from behavior, revisit the brief. Treat this as an iterative planning practice, not a promise of rankings or conversions.
